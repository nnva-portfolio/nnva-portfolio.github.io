The 30-second version

Designathon is a 24-hour UI/UX competition run by Lollypop Design Studio. About 105 young designers, one day, and feedback from experienced judges.

My team made HeoCare, a smart barn assistant that uses AI to help small pig farmers spot disease early, at low cost.

01

The problem: a strong industry that breaks easily

Our team worked on pig farming in Vietnam. It feeds millions of families, and one disease can undo years of work.

Our slides opened with the scale: about 3.1 million households raise pigs, with around 30 million pigs in total, which puts Vietnam in the top 6 in the world (source on our slide: the Government e-newspaper).

3.1Mhouseholds raise pigs
30Mpigs in total, top 6 in the world
6M+pigs lost to disease or culling since African swine fever was first announced, 19 Feb 2019

The insight

Pig farming is the backbone of Vietnamese agriculture, yet it is very fragile to disease.

We named three causes: small households produce more than 80% of fresh pork (Nga et al., 2014), most farmers lack knowledge, and management is not coordinated.

01Experience, not data

Farming relies on experience and lacks scientific data.

02No health tracking

There is no systematic way to track the health of each pig.

03Fear of outbreaks

Farmers live in fear of a sudden outbreak.

04Slow, error-prone records

Record keeping takes time and is easy to get wrong.

Problem statement

More than 80% of output comes from about 3 million small households that lack scientific tools to detect and prevent disease early. The result is the loss of millions of pigs and financial risk worth tens of thousands of billions of VND.

02

Who we designed for

We gave the problem a face: Uncle Tu, a farmer who knows his pigs well and has no tool to watch all of them at once.

Uncle Tu

  • 48
  • owner of a family pig farm
  • 50 – 100 pigs

Traits

Plenty of experience, manages by instinct. Lives in the countryside, not very tech-savvy but uses a basic smartphone.

Behavior

Watches the pigs in person, writes illnesses and vaccinations in a notebook; when a pig is sick he asks someone experienced or calls the vet.

Pain points

Fear of disease, no tool to track each pig’s health, hard to manage alone.

Goals

Keep the herd healthy, worry less about outbreaks, and have a steady income for the family.

In his words

We wrote two needs as a farmer would say them.

As a pig farmer

“I want to get simple, easy-to-understand warnings about signs of disease, so I can isolate, treat and stop it from spreading in the herd.”

As a pig farmer

“I want a visual system that shows the herd’s health in real time, so one look tells me how the whole herd is doing.”

How might we

We turned those needs into four questions to design from.

HMW 1

How might we design a simple experience that most farmers can reach?

HMW 2

How might we help most farmers use AI in agriculture?

HMW 3

How might we diagnose a pig’s illness in time?

HMW 4

How might we turn health data (eating, breathing, movement) into charts that are easy to read?

03

HeoCare: a barn assistant in your pocket

HeoCare is a smart barn assistant. It uses AI to detect and stop disease early, at low cost.

The app opens on what matters most: an alert about a barn that needs a check. From there the farmer can look at the pigs in trouble, ask the AI assistant by voice or text, and read a short summary with treatment steps.

Our last slides were about outcomes, limits and what comes next.

Limitations

Two limits we named in our deck.

People and environment

The app works only if farmers follow biosecurity steps. Households without a closed process may weaken the prevention.

Accuracy of the core AI

AI is strong at sound and images, but a 100% accurate diagnosis from early signs such as a cough is still hard. Measuring temperature with a camera and no special sensor is also limited.

Future plan

Two directions our deck listed for what could come after the competition.

Government and specialist bodies

Build relationships with the relevant ministries, such as the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development, to integrate data.

Other livestock

Reuse the core AI (behavior and sound analysis). Spotting abnormal signs could be retrained for chickens, ducks and cattle.

08

My 24 hours

The deck shows what we made. This part is about how it was to make it.

Who did what

There were eight of us: seven teammates and our buddy. We split the work into five parts: branding, research, UI, slides and the pitch. I took the research, first for choosing the topic, then for the features, and after that the UI design. Everyone also researched one part of the topic themselves, so when the judges asked about a part, the person who researched it answered.

The most lost I felt

The brief felt huge. Two hours after we got it, at around 10 in the morning, the seven of us and our buddy still had no idea which way to go. I looked around and some teams were already working on branding, so I was really worried. We sat in a circle, had a quick huddle and talked it through until we had a direction.

Once the topic was set I dived into research. At 9 in the evening I was still deep in research on the features and had not drawn a single screen yet. That is the 21:02 phone on the floor in the photo above.

The pitch

At the end we went up to answer the judges’ questions. I was very nervous, but I had done the research properly, so I could answer. We did not win a prize.

What I took with me

I met the people in my team and worked with them, and that gave me a lot of inspiration. Everyone is on a different path and everyone is very good, so there was a lot to learn. And one thing stayed with me: when you are asked hard questions, solid research is what you can stand on.
